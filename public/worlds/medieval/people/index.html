Medieval Kingdom · People

People of the Medieval Kingdom

History Who lived in a medieval town? Most people in the Middle Ages were farmers, but towns were full of bakers, masons, carpenters, merchants, monks and nuns, and children who helped with the work. Clothes were made of wool and linen, and bright dyes cost more.

  • Baker's daughter

    Carrying fresh loaves

    Children often worked in the family trade. A baker's family rose before dawn to light the oven, and children carried loaves to customers and to market. Bread was so important that its weight and price were set by law.

    Where to find them: Bakery, Market hall, Windmill

    Find them on the map

  • Squire

    A knight in training

    A squire was a teenager training to become a knight. He looked after a knight's horse and armor, practiced with sword, shield and lance, and followed his knight to tournaments and to war.

    Where to find them: Training yard, Tournament field, Knight statue

    Find them on the map

  • Child with a toy horse

    Playing in the street

    Medieval children played with balls, spinning tops, dolls, marbles and little toy knights and horses. Archaeologists have found toys made of wood, clay and even metal in the mud of old towns like London.

    Where to find them: Peasant cottage, Village well, Orchard

    Find them on the map

  • Lady with a prayer book

    Walking to church at dusk

    Wealthy families owned books of hours: small, beautifully painted prayer books with prayers for each part of the day. They were often the first books children learned to read from. A lantern with a candle lit the way home after dark.

    Where to find them: Scriptorium, Stone chapel, Manor house

    Find them on the map

  • Stonemason

    Carrying a cut stone

    Masons cut and laid the stones of castles, churches and bridges. Master masons also designed buildings, much like architects today. Many masons cut their own small mark into the stones they shaped.

    Where to find them: Building materials, Treadwheel crane, Abbey

    Find them on the map

  • Carpenter

    Carrying oak beams

    Carpenters built the timber frames of houses and halls, roofs, floors, carts and the scaffolding builders stood on. They joined beams with wooden pegs instead of nails, using joints that could last for centuries.

    Where to find them: Town house, Great hall, Building materials
