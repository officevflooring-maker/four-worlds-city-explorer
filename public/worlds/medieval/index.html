About the Middle Ages

The Middle Ages, or medieval period, is the time in European history between the end of the Roman Empire in the west, around AD 500, and about 1500. Life changed a great deal over those thousand years, and from place to place, so this town mixes things from different centuries, mostly from about 1100 to 1450.

Castle and defense

How a medieval town and its lord kept people safe.

  • Royal castleA fortified home for a king or lord: thick stone walls, towers and a gate that could be shut tight.
  • GatehouseA fortified gate between two towers, where guards decided who could come in and collected tolls.
  • WatchtowerA lookout where watchmen kept an eye on the roads and the town, day and night.
  • Great hallThe big room at the heart of a castle or manor, where the household ate, met and held court.
  • Knight statueA stone knight with a shield: a reminder of the soldiers on horseback who served kings and lords.

Churches and abbeys

Places of prayer that were also places of learning, care and work.

  • AbbeyA community of monks or nuns who lived by a rule of prayer and work, with a great church at its center.
  • Stone chapelA small stone church where the village gathered on Sundays and feast days.
  • Bell towerA tall tower whose great bell told the town the time, called people together and warned of fire.

Learning and books

Schools, universities and the workshops where books were made by hand.

  • Village schoolA small school where boys learned Latin, reading, writing and singing, often taught by a priest.
  • ScriptoriumThe room where monks copied books by hand, letter by letter, before printing was invented.
  • University hallA college where students from many lands studied law, medicine, the arts and the Bible.

Markets, crafts and guilds

Where goods were made, bought and sold, and how workers organized.

  • Market crossA stone cross in the market square that marked where trading happened and reminded people to deal honestly.
  • Market hallAn open hall on arches where traders sold their goods, with a meeting room upstairs.
  • BakeryA bakehouse with a big oven, where bread, the most important food of all, was baked every day.
  • Guild hallThe meeting place of a guild, a group of craftsmen or merchants who set rules for their trade.
  • BlacksmithA forge where the smith heated iron until it glowed and hammered it into tools, nails and horseshoes.
  • Merchant's houseA comfortable town house where a trader lived above the shop and stored goods below.

Farms and food

Fields, mills, orchards and gardens that fed the town.

  • WindmillA mill whose big sails caught the wind to turn heavy stones and grind grain into flour.
  • FarmsteadA family farm with chickens, a cart and strips of land in the big open fields around the village.
  • Shepherd's cottageA small stone cottage for a shepherd, who looked after the sheep whose wool made England rich.
  • OrchardRows of apple and pear trees that gave fruit to eat fresh, dry for winter or press into cider.
  • Herb gardenNeat beds of herbs grown for cooking, medicine and sweet smells, with beehives for honey and wax.

Homes

How people lived, from one-room cottages to manor houses.

  • Peasant cottageA thatched home of timber and woven sticks plastered with mud, with a vegetable patch outside.
  • Town houseA tall timber-framed house with upper floors that jut out over the street.
  • Tower houseA tall, narrow stone home built like a small fortress, with a room on each floor.
  • Manor houseThe home of the lord of the manor, the center of a farming estate and its village.

Halls, kitchens and wells

Where people ate, met, cooked and fetched water.

  • Feast hallA hall with a long table set for a feast, when a lord or guild celebrated with music and many dishes.
  • Kitchen and hearthAn open cooking hearth with a big pot. Big houses often built their kitchens apart, in case of fire.
  • Village wellA stone well with a roof, rope and bucket, where people drew water every day.

Tournaments and training

Where knights and townspeople practiced and competed.

  • Tournament fieldA field with a fence down the middle where knights jousted, charging at each other with blunted lances.
  • Archery buttsA practice ground with straw targets where townsmen trained with the longbow.
  • Training yardA fenced yard where squires practiced with wooden swords, shields and a spinning target called a quintain.

Building the town

The machines and materials medieval builders used.

  • Treadwheel craneA wooden crane powered by people walking inside a giant wheel, used to lift stone for castles and cathedrals.
  • Building materialsCut stone, oak beams and bundles of thatch: the materials that built castles, churches and homes.

Vehicles and getting around

How people and goods move around the city.

  • Covered wagonA four-wheeled wagon with a cloth cover, pulled by horses, that carried goods and travelers between towns.
  • Horse and riderA rider on horseback: the fastest way to travel in medieval Europe, for knights, messengers and nobles.
  • Hay cartAn ox cart piled high with hay, cut in summer to feed the animals through the winter.
  • Donkey cartA small cart pulled by a donkey, loaded with wooden barrels of ale, wine or salted fish.
