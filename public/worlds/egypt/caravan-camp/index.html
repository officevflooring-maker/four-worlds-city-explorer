History Into the desert

The deserts on either side of the Nile held things Egypt needed: copper and turquoise in Sinai, gold and hard stone in the eastern desert. Kings sent expeditions of hundreds or even thousands of people, with donkeys carrying water and food. They dug wells, camped at night and carved their names on rocks along the way.

History The land of Punt

Around 1470 BC, Queen Hatshepsut sent ships down the Red Sea to the land of Punt, probably on the coast of East Africa. Carvings at her temple at Deir el-Bahari show the travelers bringing home incense trees, ebony, gold and baboons.

Art In this artwork

The illustration shows a striped canopy over cushions and rugs around a small fire. Travelers' camps were usually much simpler, but tents do appear in pictures of Egyptian army camps.

Related buildings

  • MarketStalls of food, cloth and pottery where people swapped goods. Egyptians bartered: they traded goods for goods instead of paying with coins.
  • WatchtowerA slim lookout tower. Egypt guarded its borders with forts and watchposts, especially in the south, toward Nubia.
  • Camel riderA camel with a rider and colorful saddle bags. Camels are perfect for deserts, but they came to Egypt late: for most of its history, donkeys carried the loads.
