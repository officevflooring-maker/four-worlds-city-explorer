People of the Green World

In the painting The people of this forest city are painted characters, but their jobs are real. Rangers, gardeners, woodworkers, hikers and forest-school children look after forests, water and wildlife in many places today.

  • Neighbor in linen

    Walking to the market

    Linen is woven from the stems of the flax plant, one of the oldest cloths people make. Flax fibers about 30,000 years old have been found in a cave in Georgia, in the Caucasus.

    Where to find them: Harvest market hall, Forest cottage, Rain garden court

    Find them on the map

  • Forest ranger

    Carrying a coil of rope

    Rangers look after parks and forests. They keep trails safe, watch for fires, count wildlife and teach visitors. The first national park, Yellowstone, was set up in the United States in 1872.

    Where to find them: Tiny forest, Stag crossing, Spring circle

    Find them on the map

  • Forest-school child

    Off to learn outdoors

    At a forest school, children learn outside in all weather: building shelters, finding plants and animals and telling stories. Forest kindergartens began in Denmark in the 1950s.

    Where to find them: Forest school, Pollinator meadow, Canopy climbing course

    Find them on the map

  • Hiker

    With a walking stick and a bedroll

    Hikers follow marked trails, like the Appalachian Trail in the eastern United States, about 2,200 miles long. Good hikers stay on the path and carry their trash home.

    Where to find them: Forest training grove, Waterfall tower, Star dome

    Find them on the map

  • Older gardener

    Watering the gardens

    In shared gardens, neighbors grow food together. Watering early in the morning or in the evening saves water, because less of it dries up in the sun.

    Where to find them: Rain garden court, Seed library, Pollinator meadow

    Find them on the map

  • Walker in a wool cap

    Out for a walk with a stick

    Studies link time among trees and green spaces with less stress. Gentle paths and benches help older people enjoy them too.

    Where to find them: Forest training grove, Council terrace, Open-air theater

    Find them on the map

  • Woodworker

    Carrying a bundle of wood

    Wood is a material that grows back when foresters plant new trees. Woodworkers build houses and furniture, and repair things so they last longer.

    Where to find them: Repair workshop, Timber family house, Living crane

    Find them on the map

  • Apple picker

    Carrying the harvest

    An apple tree can bear fruit for many decades. Food grown close to home has a shorter trip to the table.

    Where to find them: Harvest market hall, Canopy market, Rain garden court

    Find them on the map
