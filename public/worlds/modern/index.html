Homes and neighbourhoods

Where people live, from houses with yards to towers in the sky.

  • Family houseA detached house with a porch and garden, built for one household. It is the most common home in many countries.
  • Suburban houseA house with a driveway and garage on the edge of a city, designed around travel by car.
  • Older brick houseAn older home that can be repaired and upgraded to use far less energy, instead of being knocked down.
  • Apartment buildingA building of several floors with many homes stacked together, sharing walls, stairs and services.
  • Residential towerA tall glass tower of apartments that fits hundreds of homes onto a small plot of land.
  • Shops with homes aboveA building with shops on the street and homes upstairs, keeping streets lively day and night.

Government and public services

How a town makes decisions and looks after people's health.

  • Town hallThe home of local government, where elected leaders meet and residents get public services.
  • Capitol buildingA domed building where elected lawmakers for a state or country meet to debate and pass laws.
  • Health clinicA neighbourhood clinic where people see a doctor or nurse, get vaccines and stay healthy before small problems grow.
  • Clock towerA tall tower with a clock that everyone could see, from the days before every person carried the time.

Streets, water and safety

The systems most people only notice when they stop working.

  • Streets and crosswalksStreets are a town's largest public space. Their design decides whether walking, cycling and crossing feel safe.
  • Water systemHidden pipes, pumps and treatment plants bring clean water to every tap and carry dirty water safely away.
  • LighthouseA tower with a powerful light that warns ships of rocks and guides them safely into harbour.
  • Fire lookout towerA tall tower in a forest or on a hill where a watcher scans the land for smoke from wildfires.

Learning and culture

Places to learn, explore and share ideas, free or nearly free.

  • Public libraryFree books, internet, study space and help for everyone, with no one asked to pay at the door.
  • SchoolA building where children learn, make friends and get support, and often a hub for the whole community.
  • Science centre and observatoryA museum where visitors learn by doing experiments, with a telescope dome for looking at the night sky.
  • Cultural centreA place where a community shares its traditions, art, food, language and history with neighbours and visitors.

Work and shopping

Where people earn a living and get the things they need.

  • Corner storeA small shop within walking distance selling food and everyday items, a lifeline for many neighbourhoods.
  • Office buildingA building of desks, meeting rooms and computers where many people do their jobs.
  • SkyscraperA very tall building made possible by steel frames and safe elevators, and a symbol of the modern city.
  • WarehouseA large shed where goods are stored, sorted and sent out, the hidden link between factories and shops.

Gathering and community

Spaces that bring neighbours together and help them remember.

  • Community centreA shared building for classes, clubs, meetings, celebrations and support, open to the whole neighbourhood.
  • Public square and fountainAn open square with a fountain, seating and flowers where people meet, rest and celebrate.
  • Place of worshipA building where people of a faith gather to pray and celebrate, and which often serves the wider community too.
  • Outdoor gathering circleA ring of benches around a fire pit in a park, a simple place for stories, music and outdoor lessons.
  • MemorialA monument that helps a community remember people and events from its past.
  • Public artStatues, sculptures and murals in public places that anyone can enjoy, free of charge.

Parks, sport and play

Green space, games and exercise, for health and for fun.

  • City parkTrees, grass, paths and water in the middle of town: free space to play, relax and cool down.
  • Treehouse playgroundA play space with climbing, hiding and building, where children take healthy risks and use their imagination.
  • Sports fieldAn open field with goals and lights where people play football and other team sports.
  • StadiumA huge venue where tens of thousands of fans watch sport and concerts together.
  • Recreation centreA building with gym equipment and classes where people of every age can exercise, whatever the weather.
  • Community gardenShared land where neighbours grow vegetables, fruit and flowers together.

Building the town

The machines and materials that make modern buildings possible.

  • Tower craneA tall crane that lifts steel, concrete and glass to the upper floors of new buildings.
  • Building materialsBricks, timber, concrete and steel each have strengths, costs and effects on the planet.
