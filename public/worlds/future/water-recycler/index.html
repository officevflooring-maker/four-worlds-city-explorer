Water recycler

Also called: closed-loop water

A system that cleans and reuses almost every drop of water, including sweat and urine, because water is too precious to waste.

Purpose
Reuse water again and again
ISS record
About 98% of water recovered (2023)
Sources of water
Sinks, showers, sweat, breath, urine
Why it matters
Water is heavy to launch

Real world Why recycle water?

Every kilogram launched into space costs a lot, and each person needs several litres of water a day for drinking, food and washing. Instead of bringing new water, space stations recycle it.

On the International Space Station, humidity from breath and sweat is collected from the air, and urine is distilled and filtered. In 2023 NASA announced that, with a new brine processor, the station could recover about 98 percent of the water crews bring aboard.

Real world How the system works

  • Collect: water from sinks, condensed humidity and urine.
  • Distil: urine is boiled at low pressure and the vapour is collected.
  • Filter: water passes through filters and beds that remove impurities.
  • Clean: a high-temperature reactor breaks down remaining contaminants.
  • Check: sensors test the water before anyone drinks it.

Possible future A future fountain

In a Moon or Mars settlement, a fountain could be the visible end of this system: a reminder that every drop has been cleaned and reused many times. Water from local ice could top up the system to replace the small amount lost.

Key words

Closed loop
A system that reuses its materials over and over.
Distillation
Boiling a liquid and collecting the vapour to purify it.
Brine
Very salty leftover water.
