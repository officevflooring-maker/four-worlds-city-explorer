City walls and towers

Latin: moenia, turris

Strong walls with towers and gates protected Roman cities, including Rome itself in later centuries.

History What it was

Walls protected cities from attack and let guards control who came in and out through the gates. Rome had early walls, but as the empire grew strong and its borders far away, the city spread far beyond them and for centuries had no need for new defenses.

In the 3rd century AD, the empire faced invasions and civil wars. Between AD 271 and 275, the emperor Aurelian built a new wall around Rome, about 19 kilometers long, with a square tower roughly every 30 meters.

History How it worked

Towers stuck out from the wall so that defenders could shoot along its face at attackers trying to climb it. Gates were the weakest points, so they were usually flanked by two strong towers. Walls were later made taller and stronger as threats grew.

History Places you can still see

Long stretches of the Aurelian Walls still surround central Rome. The Porta San Sebastiano, where the Via Appia leaves the city, is one of the best-preserved gates and houses a small museum of the walls. In Britain, Hadrian's Wall, begun in AD 122, marked the empire's northern frontier.
