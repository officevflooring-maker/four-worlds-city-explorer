How to read these pages

Each page has three kinds of section. Imagined sections describe the fantasy building. Real world sections describe science, places and technology that exist today, with sources. Possible future sections describe ideas still being researched, which may or may not work.

Living buildings

Imagined buildings grown from living trees and plants, and the real science of building with them.

  • Great tree hallAn imagined hall grown from a living tree. In the real world, people already shape living roots and trunks into bridges and buildings.
  • Sunlight hallAn imagined hall that feeds on sunlight like a leaf. Real buildings already grow algae in their walls to make heat and fuel.
  • Treetop villageAn imagined village of homes and bridges among the branches, built so lightly that the trees stay healthy.
  • Tree tower homeAn imagined tower wrapped in trees. Real "vertical forests" plant hundreds of trees on the balconies of city apartment blocks.
  • Living craneAn imagined crane woven from vines. In the real world, fast-growing bamboo is strong enough to build scaffolding and houses.

Homes in the forest

Cosy homes that stay comfortable with the help of earth, wood, plants and air.

  • Hillside homeA home tucked into a grassy hill. Real earth-sheltered homes use the soil around them to stay cool in summer and warm in winter.
  • Timber family houseAn imagined house carved from wood. Real engineered timber now builds homes and towers that store carbon instead of releasing it.
  • Breathing spireAn imagined tower that breathes like a termite mound, pulling cool air in and letting warm air out with no machines.
  • Green-roof cottageA tall cottage crowned with plants. Real green roofs soak up rain, cool buildings and feed insects.
  • Forest cottageAn imagined cottage made of earth, straw, stone and wood. Real natural building uses these same simple materials.
  • Leafy cottageAn imagined cottage wrapped in climbing plants. Real research suggests that seeing nature helps people feel and heal better.
  • Mushroom cottageA playful toadstool home. Real scientists grow building blocks and packaging from mycelium, the root-like threads of fungi.

Water and energy

Springs, wetlands, falling water and the warmth of the earth.

  • Cleansing fountainAn imagined fountain that cleans water as it flows. Real "constructed wetlands" use plants and microbes to purify wastewater.
  • Rain garden courtAn imagined courtyard garden that drinks the rain. Real rain gardens catch storm water and stop flooding.
  • Spring circleAn imagined ring of stones guarding a sacred spring. Real cities save billions by protecting the land where their water comes from.
  • Crystal waterfallAn imagined glowing waterfall. Real micro-hydro systems turn falling water into electricity for homes and villages.
  • Warm gathering circleAn imagined meeting place warmed by a glow from the earth. Real geothermal energy heats most homes in Iceland.

Wildlife and wild places

Making room for bees, deer, forests and night skies.

  • Pollinator meadowFlowers planted for bees, butterflies and other insects that pollinate the crops we eat.
  • Tiny forestA patch of trees left wild in the middle of the city. Real "tiny forests" plant dense native woods in spaces the size of a tennis court.
  • Stag crossingAn imagined monument to the forest's animals. Real wildlife bridges let deer, bears and wolves cross busy roads safely.
  • Deer shrineAn imagined shrine to a gentle deer. In the real world, "rewilding" lets nature restore itself on land that was farmed.
  • Star domeAn imagined observatory under a sky full of stars. Real dark-sky places reduce light pollution so people and wildlife can see the night.
  • Glowing towerAn imagined tower that glows like a firefly. Real scientists have already made living plants that glow in the dark.

Learning and gathering

Places to learn outdoors, store seeds, watch the seasons and make decisions together.

  • Seed libraryAn imagined library inside a hollow tree, storing seeds instead of books. Real seed banks protect the world's plants for the future.
  • Forest schoolAn imagined school among the trees. Real forest schools take children outdoors to learn in every season.
  • Seasons towerAn imagined tower where elves watch the weather and the seasons. Real scientists track when flowers bloom to see how climate is changing.
  • Council terraceAn imagined terrace where elves decide together how to share the forest. Real research shows communities can manage shared resources well.
  • Palace of the long futureAn imagined palace where leaders must think a hundred years ahead. Real governments have begun to create laws for future generations.
  • Forest training groveAn imagined archery grove among the trees. Real research explores how time in forests may lower stress and lift mood.
  • Canopy climbing courseAn imagined climbing course in the treetops. Real scientists use walkways, ropes and cranes to study life in the forest canopy.
  • Open-air theatreAn imagined theatre among the trees. Real open-air theatres use the shape of hills and stone to carry sound without microphones.

Making and trading

Markets, workshops and materials that waste little and borrow ideas from nature.

  • Canopy marketMarket stalls under giant mushroom-shaped canopies. Real shade structures, and real trees, cool hot city squares.
  • Harvest market hallAn imagined market where nothing is wasted. Real towns cut waste by selling local food and composting what is left.
  • Repair workshopAn imagined workshop where nothing is thrown away. Real repair cafés and well-managed forests keep materials in use.
  • Nature's materialsStone, timber and crystals for elven builders, and the real inventions engineers have copied from nature.
