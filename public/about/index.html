About this project

Four Worlds City Explorer is a free educational and creative website. It uses illustrated cities to help visitors notice how cities work and think about what makes a good place to live.

What is real and what is imagined

Ancient Rome is a historical setting. Each of its 35 building pages keeps history separate from the artist's interpretation and lists the sources it is based on. The illustrations are not exact reconstructions; photos of real Roman sites show what survives today.

The modern town is imagined, but its 36 pages explain real buildings and systems: why towns need them, how they are built and who they serve.

The green elven world and robotic future are imagined and speculative. They don't describe real places, real cultures or predictions. Each page uses labels so you always know what you are reading:

  • History and Real world facts about the past and about real places, technology and research, with sources.
  • Imagined ideas that belong to the fantasy world.
  • Possible future ideas being researched or proposed, which may or may not happen.

Privacy

This site has no accounts, forms, comments, chat, ads, analytics or tracking. It sets no cookies and loads nothing from other websites. Your web host may keep standard server logs.

Accessibility

Every building is listed in text as well as on the map. The site works with a keyboard and screen readers, respects reduced-motion settings, and has no autoplay or flashing effects.

Artwork and reuse

All illustrations of the four worlds are free for everyone to use, under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0) licence. You can copy, print, change, share and use them for any purpose, including in lessons, books, websites and commercial projects. The only condition is to credit them, for example: "Illustration: Four Worlds City Explorer".

Save or print any of them from the Artwork page.

Photos of real Roman places come from Wikimedia Commons and are public domain or shared under Creative Commons licences. Each photo is credited where it appears, and all licences are listed on the photo credits page.
