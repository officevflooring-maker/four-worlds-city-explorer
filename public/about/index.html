About this project

The Atlas of Cities is a free educational and creative website. It uses illustrated cities to help visitors notice how cities work and think about what makes a good place to live.

What is real and what is imagined

Ancient Egypt and ancient Rome are historical settings. Each of their building pages keeps history separate from the artist's interpretation and lists the sources it is based on. The illustrations are not exact reconstructions; photos of real sites show what survives today.

The modern town is imagined, but its pages explain real buildings, vehicles and systems: why towns need them, how they are built and who they serve.

The Green World and robotic future are imagined and speculative. They don't describe real places, real cultures or predictions. Each page uses labels so you always know what you are reading:

  • History and Real world facts about the past and about real places, technology and research, with sources.
  • Imagined ideas that belong to the fantasy world.
  • Possible future ideas being researched or proposed, which may or may not happen.

The Heavenly City is imagined too, inspired by the Bible's picture of the heavenly city. As you explore it, its Gospel sections tell the story of Jesus in 35 parts, as the Bible tells it, with chapter and verse, and its Bible sections share more words from Scripture. A few Green World pages also share Bible verses about God the Creator. Bible quotations are from the Berean Standard Bible, which is in the public domain.

Privacy

This site has no accounts, forms, comments, chat, ads, analytics or tracking. It sets no cookies and loads nothing from other websites. Your web host may keep standard server logs.

Passport stamps, quiz scores and the city you build are saved only in your own browser and never sent anywhere. You can clear them on the passport page.

Things in the shop are paid for on our store's website, under that store's own privacy policy. This site never sees payment or personal details.

Accessibility

Every building is listed in text as well as on the map. The site works with a keyboard and screen readers, respects reduced-motion settings, and has no autoplay or flashing effects.

Artwork and reuse

All illustrations of the six worlds are free for everyone to use, under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0) license. You can copy, print, change, share and use them for any purpose, including in lessons, books, websites and commercial projects. The only condition is to credit them, for example: "Illustration: The Atlas of Cities".

Save or print any of them from the Artwork page.

The teacher guides, worksheets, glossary and question bank are free to print and share for teaching. Please credit The Atlas of Cities.

Photos of real places come from Wikimedia Commons and are public domain or shared under Creative Commons licenses. Each photo is credited where it appears, and all licenses are listed on the photo credits page.
