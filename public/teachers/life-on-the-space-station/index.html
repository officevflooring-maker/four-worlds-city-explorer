How do astronauts live on the International Space Station?

Grades
3-12
Time
10-minute read
Subjects
Science

Free to print and share for teaching, in a classroom or a homeschool. Please credit The Atlas of Cities.

Quick answer

On the International Space Station, oxygen is made by splitting water with electricity, about 98% of water is recycled (including sweat and urine), food is mostly packaged and shipped from Earth, and astronauts exercise about two hours a day to protect their bones and muscles.

Air

The station makes oxygen by electrolysis: splitting water into hydrogen and oxygen. Carbon dioxide breathed out by the crew is removed by special filters. Fans keep air moving, because in weightlessness warm air does not rise.

Water

Water is collected from the air (breath and sweat) and from urine, then distilled, filtered and checked. In 2023 NASA announced the station could recover about 98% of the water crews bring aboard.

Food

Most food is shipped from Earth in packages. Astronauts have also grown and eaten fresh lettuce and other plants in NASA's Veggie plant growth system, first eating space-grown lettuce in 2015.

Exercise and health

Without gravity, bones and muscles weaken. Astronauts exercise about two hours a day on a treadmill (with a harness holding them down), a bicycle and a resistance machine.

Sleep

Astronauts sleep in small crew cabins, in sleeping bags attached to the wall so they do not float around.

Questions students and teachers ask

How do astronauts get oxygen on the ISS?

By splitting water into hydrogen and oxygen using electricity, a process called electrolysis.

Do astronauts drink their own urine?

Their urine is cleaned and recycled into drinking water that is purer than much tap water on Earth.

Why do astronauts exercise so much?

In weightlessness, bones and muscles weaken quickly, so about two hours of exercise a day helps keep them strong.
