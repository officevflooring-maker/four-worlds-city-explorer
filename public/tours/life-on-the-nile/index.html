Ancient Egypt · Guided tour

Life on the Nile

The river, food, writing and great monuments.

Stops
8
Time
20 to 30 minutes
Grades
3-9

Tour stops

  1. 1. Nile sailing boat
  2. 2. Palm grove
  3. 3. Granary
  4. 4. Market
  5. 5. Scribe school
  6. 6. Hall of columns
  7. 7. Obelisk
  8. 8. Pyramid
  9. Think it over

  1. Stop 1

    Nile sailing boat

    The Nile is our highway. Boats sail south with the wind and float north with the current, carrying grain, stone and people.

    Why might a river be easier to travel on than a road?

    Read the full page about the nile sailing boat

  2. Stop 2

    Palm grove

    Channels carry the river's water to fields and palm groves. Date palms give us fruit, shade, rope, baskets and roof beams.

    How many different things can one plant give a city?

    Read the full page about the palm grove

  3. Stop 3

    Granary

    After the harvest, grain goes into domed granaries. Full granaries mean bread and beer all year, even after a poor flood.

    Why would a city store food for a bad year?

    Read the full page about the granary

  4. Stop 4

    Market

    There are no coins here. At the market people swap goods: sandals for grain, a basket for fish.

    What would be hard about trading without money?

    Read the full page about the market

  5. Stop 5

    Scribe school

    This is where I learned to write, after years of copying signs. Scribes keep the records that keep the city fair.

    Why do cities need people who can read and write?

    Read the full page about the scribe school

  6. Stop 6

    Hall of columns

    In the temple, giant painted columns hold up the roof, like the Great Hypostyle Hall at Karnak, which has 134 of them.

    How do you think builders raised such heavy columns?

    Read the full page about the hall of columns

  7. Stop 7

    Obelisk

    Each obelisk was cut from a single piece of granite and set up in pairs at the temple gates.

    How might people have moved a stone this big without engines?

    Read the full page about the obelisk

  8. Stop 8

    Pyramid

    The Great Pyramid at Giza, a tomb for a pharaoh, was the tallest building on Earth for about 3,800 years.

    Why do you think rulers wanted such huge tombs?

    Read the full page about the pyramid

Think it over

  1. What would happen to this city if the Nile flood failed?
  2. Which job would you choose: farmer, scribe, builder or boat captain? Why?
  3. Which Egyptian idea do we still use today?
